## Current Synthesis

### Cracks at the margins, 17 Apr – 1 May 2026 — bazaar, Bahá'ís, conscripts, oil, IRIB, Afghans

Pulling on the under-covered seams: (a) Tehran Bazaar — historically the single most loaded commercial-class signal in Iranian regime collapse — shut down nationwide on 29 Apr in protest of the rial collapse, after merchant gatherings on 20 and 27 Apr (ISNA/Fars/IRNA on the regime side, Jahan-Sanat on the dissent side). (b) Bahá'í minority pressure intensified: the Universal House of Justice publicly named two Bahá'í cousins, Peyvand and Borna Naimi, as facing execution; three Bahá'í women jailed in Kerman; sustained detentions in Mashhad, Shiraz, Isfahan. (c) Conscription strain visible in policy: on 28 Apr the regime announced fresh fines for absent or runaway conscripts — a tightening rarely reached for unless desertion is real. (d) Oil dark-fleet under sustained pressure: 24 Apr Treasury sanctions on ~20 companies and 19 ships including Hengli (Dalian); 25 Apr CENTCOM interception of M/V Sevan; tankers systematically going dark per Bloomberg, clustering shy of the blockade line off Chabahar. (e) IRIB compromise public: an IRIB-affiliated outlet acknowledged a 'third American-Zionist attack' on state TV on 27 Apr; France 24 (mirrored) reports state TV was hijacked to broadcast a Reza Pahlavi speech ~29 Apr — likely Predatory Sparrow. (f) Afghan migrants as the regime's pressure-release valve: per the UN, >120,000 deported in Q1 2026, with single-day expulsions of 6,000–7,000 reported on 18-19 Apr — classic crisis scapegoating. (g) Hijab posture re-tightened: IRGC deputy commander warned women on 22 Apr that hijab is 'God's own command'; Iran International framed the post-war regime as 'returning to the 60s' on gender. (h) A second elite-defection signal: a senior reformist publicly called for 'courage in flexibility, not endless confrontation' on 21 Apr — paralleling Rouhani's 'great change'. (i) Friday-prayer optics: regime mouthpiece SNN aired an unusually loud 1 May line about 'not emptying the streets' — its emphasis itself diagnostic. Together these sit underneath the headline cracks (command vacuum, currency collapse, labour, infrastructure, clergy defection, students, prisons, pharma, cyber, Kurdistan) — i.e., the regime's external valves (oil, refugees, broadcasting) and its internal coercion levers (Bahá'í minority, hijab, conscripts) are simultaneously stressed, while the bazaar is starting to blink red.

Limitations: Two-week window. Persian on-the-ground signal is dampened by the national internet blackout; reliance on diaspora outlets (Iran International, Hengaw, Kayhan London) and regime-side counter-mouthpieces (Jam-e Jam, SNN, Sharq Daily) is mitigated by recording each side's framing alongside the other. Bazaar closure scope (Tehran-only vs. multi-city) and IRIB-hijack provenance (Predatory Sparrow attribution) carry the largest residual uncertainty.

## Open Questions

- Does the 29 Apr Tehran Bazaar shutdown spread (Tabriz, Mashhad, Isfahan bazaars) and persist for >5 days, the way 1978 bazaar strikes did?
- Will the Naimi cousins be executed, sentenced to long imprisonment, or released as part of post-war optics?
- Is the new conscript-fine policy a response to mass desertion, or a routine fee adjustment? What is the actual desertion rate post-war?
- Can the shadow fleet sustain Iran's ~$30B/year oil-export volume against a hardening US blockade + Treasury sanctions, or does export volume collapse below the regime's fiscal break-even?
- Will the IRGC's 'God's command' hijab line trigger a fresh phase of Zhina-style street defiance, or is post-war fatigue / bandwidth elsewhere keeping women's resistance latent?
- Does the regime publish any actual conscript-desertion or absentee count in May 2026, or does it only continue announcing penalties? If only the latter, that itself is a signal that the gap between announced policy and ground reality is widening.
- Does Radan's pattern of repeated unity-insistence shift to specific named-faction conciliation language in May, or harden into more explicit threat language — and either way, does any provincial police commander break from the central messaging?
