## Current Synthesis

### Cracks in the Islamic Republic, v43 - Hiring data confirms the digital labor freeze

The forty-third pass upgrades v42's weaker hiring-demand lead with source-near labor-platform metrics. IranTalent-linked reporting says hiring demand fell 57% after internet disruptions, SMEs fell 74% in the first three weeks of the international-internet cutoff, and spring specialist opportunities were down 78% year-over-year from a prior comparable average near 65k opportunities/month. JobVision data independently shows the employer side withdrawing more than the worker side: during the 21-plus-day Jan/Feb cutoff, new ads fell 64% while applications fell 34%; after partial restoration, applications were only about 8% below baseline but ads remained 30% lower, with internet-dependent roles still deeply depressed. A later JobVision report says more than 318k resumes were submitted in one day, 50% above the previous record, while ads were down year-over-year. Nasr-linked reporting adds a one-fifth layoff-risk estimate for digital-company workers, and local Sanandaj reporting gives ground-level corroboration that some specialist workers have become jobless. The state response remains credit and selective access: a 2k-billion-toman loan package and freelancer/approved-business connectivity efforts, not public restoration. Net: v43 still lacks a national unemployment count, but it converts the labor shock from anecdote into a platform-measured hiring freeze.

Limitations: The central remaining gap is realized unemployment. IranTalent and JobVision measure hiring opportunities, ads, resumes, and applications, not unemployment-insurance registrations. The one-fifth layoff-risk figure and Sanandaj jobless reports corroborate employment damage but are not audited denominators. Causality also overlaps with war, recession, inflation, and broader uncertainty, although the role-category breakdown points strongly to the internet cutoff as the distinctive channel.

### Iran regime-stress map: 17-30 April 2026 (Persian-source-weighted)

Cracks visible across five reinforcing layers as of late Ordibehesht 1405. Layer 1 (currency / household pressure): the open-market dollar broke 180,000 toman on 29 April, a 120% rise from pre-12-day-war levels and a historic record low for the rial; BBC Persian and Iran International report households selling assets and buying on credit. Layer 2 (industrial collapse and labor unrest): Iran International reports only ~60 of 1,500 factories operating in the Tabriz Salimi estate; ~250 export/digital companies fully closed; HRANA/ILNA confirmed at least four labor protests on 29 April including steel pensioners in Esfahan and Tehran and railway workers in Dorud and Andimeshk; Tehran University staff struck 26 April; Eghtesad-e Irani describes a 'resignation domino' inside government and the head of the Plan & Budget Organization publicly apologized on 1 May for the 20% wage gap. Layer 3 (legitimacy and bazaar): on 27 April 2026 even state news agencies (ISNA/Fars/IRNA) acknowledged scattered protest gatherings inside Tehran's Grand Bazaar with chanting on Naser-Khosrow Street; Jahan-e Sanat reported partial bazaar closure on 29 April driven by exchange-rate anger. Layer 4 (regime cohesion): Reuters (28 April) and a NYT/Farnaz Fassihi report (relayed by Melliun and Straits Times) describe Mojtaba Khamenei as a 'figure of assent rather than command' while a 'board' of IRGC generals (Vahidi, Zolqadr, Rahim Safavi) makes operational calls; Radio Farda flags a Zolqadr/Jalili-vs-Qalibaf rift inside the SNSC; multiple Persian outlets describe a real Aref-Qaempanah dispute with Aref reportedly filing a resignation Pezeshkian declined to accept. Layer 5 (regime self-assessment): per Iran International (relayed by Jerusalem Post and JNS, 28 April), the SNSC itself was briefed that protests are 'inevitable,' that Iran's economy 'cannot withstand more than six to eight weeks' of continued US naval blockade, that ~2 million private-sector workers will lose jobs by end of spring, and that protests during talks could 'pose a real risk of the fall of the Islamic Republic.' What is NOT yet a crack: the visible Salmas / Langarud / Farahan night-marches are state-organized بیعت (allegiance) rallies for the new Supreme Leader, not anti-regime protests, and should not be confused with the labor/bazaar protest wave. Net read: the regime is under cumulative stress that even its own security apparatus describes in regime-collapse language; the missing ingredient versus 2022 (Mahsa) and December 2025 (Dey 1404) is a single politicized trigger event capable of bridging the still-sectoral labor/bazaar protests into a unified street movement.

### Cracks at depth, 17 Apr – 1 May 2026 — apex, axis, alloy

Three deeper-cut fissures complete the picture. APEX (the Khamenei family + aghazadeh): a Khamenei niece was arrested on 24 Apr after publicly condemning the regime, while Dominica revoked the Caribbean citizenship of a second Shamkhani-family member on 18 Apr — closing the elite hedging hatch and signalling a visible split inside the apex itself. AXIS (the proxy and external-backer architecture): Lebanese press (Lebanon24, Elsiyasa) on 29 Apr reported that Iran is reducing its support for Hezbollah, while Bloomberg (22 Apr) and RFE/RL (17 Apr) document that Xi Jinping's support has explicit limits — Iran's strategic-depth and external-rescue scenarios are simultaneously cratering. ALLOY (legitimacy in the cultural surface): an organised diaspora movement on 30 Apr–1 May demanded that FIFA ban Iran from the 2026 World Cup ('they are not representing Iran'); a global movement campaigned to halt Toomaj Salehi's execution; the Supreme Court confirmed the Abbasi family death sentences on 27 Apr; healthcare is fragile per MSF on top of 50 reported damaged hospitals; and gold/currency are in 'explosive surge' alongside US-tracked capital flight. The Zayandeh-Rud release is being pushed to late Ordibehesht — re-arming the Isfahan-farmer flashpoint at the worst possible moment. Read together with the prior 19 cracks in iran-cracks/ and iran-margins/, the regime's apex, axis, and alloy are degrading in lockstep — i.e., the failure modes are no longer single-domain.

Limitations: The Khamenei-niece arrest rests on a single second-tier wire; verify before high-confidence use. Lebanon24/Elsiyasa Hezbollah pieces are directionally clear but qualitatively-framed; magnitude unproven. JaFaJ capital-flight claim is low-tier and should be re-anchored to a Treasury OFAC release before promotion above 'medium' confidence.

### Cracks at the margins, 17 Apr – 1 May 2026 — bazaar, Bahá'ís, conscripts, oil, IRIB, Afghans

Pulling on the under-covered seams: (a) Tehran Bazaar — historically the single most loaded commercial-class signal in Iranian regime collapse — shut down nationwide on 29 Apr in protest of the rial collapse, after merchant gatherings on 20 and 27 Apr (ISNA/Fars/IRNA on the regime side, Jahan-Sanat on the dissent side). (b) Bahá'í minority pressure intensified: the Universal House of Justice publicly named two Bahá'í cousins, Peyvand and Borna Naimi, as facing execution; three Bahá'í women jailed in Kerman; sustained detentions in Mashhad, Shiraz, Isfahan. (c) Conscription strain visible in policy: on 28 Apr the regime announced fresh fines for absent or runaway conscripts — a tightening rarely reached for unless desertion is real. (d) Oil dark-fleet under sustained pressure: 24 Apr Treasury sanctions on ~20 companies and 19 ships including Hengli (Dalian); 25 Apr CENTCOM interception of M/V Sevan; tankers systematically going dark per Bloomberg, clustering shy of the blockade line off Chabahar. (e) IRIB compromise public: an IRIB-affiliated outlet acknowledged a 'third American-Zionist attack' on state TV on 27 Apr; France 24 (mirrored) reports state TV was hijacked to broadcast a Reza Pahlavi speech ~29 Apr — likely Predatory Sparrow. (f) Afghan migrants as the regime's pressure-release valve: per the UN, >120,000 deported in Q1 2026, with single-day expulsions of 6,000–7,000 reported on 18-19 Apr — classic crisis scapegoating. (g) Hijab posture re-tightened: IRGC deputy commander warned women on 22 Apr that hijab is 'God's own command'; Iran International framed the post-war regime as 'returning to the 60s' on gender. (h) A second elite-defection signal: a senior reformist publicly called for 'courage in flexibility, not endless confrontation' on 21 Apr — paralleling Rouhani's 'great change'. (i) Friday-prayer optics: regime mouthpiece SNN aired an unusually loud 1 May line about 'not emptying the streets' — its emphasis itself diagnostic. Together these sit underneath the headline cracks (command vacuum, currency collapse, labour, infrastructure, clergy defection, students, prisons, pharma, cyber, Kurdistan) — i.e., the regime's external valves (oil, refugees, broadcasting) and its internal coercion levers (Bahá'í minority, hijab, conscripts) are simultaneously stressed, while the bazaar is starting to blink red.

Limitations: Two-week window. Persian on-the-ground signal is dampened by the national internet blackout; reliance on diaspora outlets (Iran International, Hengaw, Kayhan London) and regime-side counter-mouthpieces (Jam-e Jam, SNN, Sharq Daily) is mitigated by recording each side's framing alongside the other. Bazaar closure scope (Tehran-only vs. multi-city) and IRIB-hijack provenance (Predatory Sparrow attribution) carry the largest residual uncertainty.
