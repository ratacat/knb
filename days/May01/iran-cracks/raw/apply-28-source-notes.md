## Findings

- Tehran water:
  - Persian reports quoted water officials saying pressure is reduced from around midnight until morning to reduce leaks and refill urban reservoirs.
  - Ettelaat/Vista said pressure in all Tehran neighborhoods had been noticeably reduced overnight.
  - Didbaniran/Vista quoted Tehran Water and Wastewater's manager saying national dams averaged 32 percent full, while Tehran dams were 9 percent full with Taleghan and 3 percent without Taleghan.
  - Shafaqna/Vista quoted the water spokesman saying Tehran has a 3 m3/s water deficit and that more distribution limits may be needed if consumption-management targets are not met.
  - Multiple headlines denied formal "cutoffs" or "rationing" while describing pressure management; the operational reality is rationing by pressure control.
- Karaj and Mashhad:
  - EcoIran/Vista reported Karaj dam storage at about 14 million m3, enough for roughly two weeks of Tehran drinking water; follow-up Persian reports said the dam had only moved just above dead volume and remained about 93 percent empty, with local officials warning of roughly 15 days of water at current conditions.
  - Shahrara/Vista reported the four dams supplying Mashhad had only 6 percent water and were 94 percent empty, while officials said there was no current rationing plan.
- Electricity:
  - Iran Online/IRNA quoted Deputy Energy Minister Mostafa Rajabi Mashhadi saying industries can use full electricity only until 20 Ordibehesht 1405 (10 May 2026), after which decisions will be weekly based on temperature, consumption, and production.
  - EnergyKhodro/Abaan/Roozno, summarizing an Ettelaat warning, said the Ministry of Energy had decided to quota industrial electricity from 20 Ordibehesht and warned industrial cutoffs are more dangerous for national security and employment than temporary household blackouts.
  - Shargh and AsrIran/Vista reported post-war grid damage around 58.5-60 hemats, about 2,000 affected network areas, and thousands of damaged equipment/line/substation points, while official messaging continued to claim grid stability and ask households to save electricity.

## Interpretation

The service-stress crack is now less about hidden scarcity and more about euphemism. Authorities avoid "rationing" and "cutoff" language, but their own statements describe pressure reduction, weekly industrial-power decision rules, high-consumer controls, damaged infrastructure, and consumption-management targets. The likely near-term watchpoints are May 10 for industrial electricity, daytime water cutoffs in Tehran, and whether Mashhad moves from "no rationing plan" to pressure management as summer demand rises.
