# v43 source notes - platform hiring data confirms the digital labor freeze

Observed: 2026-05-02T05:30:00Z

Question followed: `q:iran-cracks:20260502:c26ba568` - can source-near labor-market records confirm the scale of digital-economy job loss from the blackout?

Core sources:

- Zoomit, 2026-04-29 (`raw/crawl21-zoomit-irantalent-78pct-job-opportunities.txt`): reports IranTalent data showing a 78% year-over-year drop in spring job opportunities for specialists and educated workers. It says spring normally peaks for hiring, last year's comparable period averaged about 65k opportunities per month, and this year's conditions left roughly four of five opportunities absent. The article links the demand-side collapse to war, prolonged internet outage, and reduced economic activity; it also notes companies stopping or reducing hiring, widespread layoffs or suspensions, and some business activity suspended.
- TejaratNews / Zoomit, 2026-02-17 (`raw/crawl21-tejaratnews-irantalent-57pct-hiring-drop.txt`): republishes Zoomit reporting and quotes IranTalent CEO Asiyeh Hatami. It says hiring demand dropped 57% after recent internet disruptions, compared with 35% during the 12-day war, and that SMEs saw a 74% demand drop in the first three weeks of the international-internet cutoff. It also says large companies froze hiring, internet businesses were hit hardest, one major fintech saw a 70% revenue drop, and tech firms' human-resources-heavy cost structure makes payroll impossible when internet-driven revenue collapses.
- Digiato, 2026-02-17 (`raw/crawl21-digiato-jobvision-64pct-job-ads-drop.txt`): reports JobVision data showing the 21-plus-day Jan/Feb internet cutoff cut new job ads by 64% while applications fell 34%. After partial restoration, applications were only about 8% below pre-crisis levels but new job ads remained 30% lower. Internet-dependent roles were hit hardest: digital marketing/SEO ads fell 80% during the Google cutoff and remained 70% below pre-crisis; content/graphic roles fell 70% and remained 50% lower; customer-support roles fell 70% and remained 40% lower; tech roles fell 60% and remained 30% lower.
- Digiato, 2026-04-27 (`raw/crawl21-digiato-jobvision-318k-resumes.txt`): reports JobVision data that 318k-plus resumes were sent on 5 Ordibehesht 1405, a one-day record 50% above the previous record, while job ads were down compared with the same period last year. The article reads this as evidence of broad layoffs or insecurity and an unusual demand-for-work surge against reduced opportunity supply.
- Iran International, 2026-04-26 (`raw/crawl21-iranintl-nasr-one-fifth-layoff-risk.txt`): quotes Jahangir Aghazadeh, head of the internet commission at Tehran's Computer Trade Organization, saying the internet cutoff causes up to 5k billion tomans/day in damage and that about one-fifth of workers at digital companies are at risk of layoff. This is a sector estimate, not an audited workforce count.
- Digiato, 2026-02-22 (`raw/crawl21-digiato-2-hemat-support-loan.txt`): reports a Ministry of Communications / Innovation Fund / Post Bank agreement for up to 2k billion tomans in loans to damaged digital-economy businesses, at 15.33% interest, with a three-month grace period and nine-month repayment, explicitly aimed at damage compensation, preserving employment, and return to normal activity.
- Masalat / ISNA / EghtesadOnline, 2026-04-17 (`raw/crawl21-masalat-online-businesses-50pct-support.txt`): reports at least 50% income losses for some online businesses, the 2k-billion-toman support package, weekly damage assessment by ICT deputy Ehsan Chitsaz, estimated war-period losses of about 500b tomans/day in the digital-economy core and 2 hemats/day in layers two and three, plans to work with the Statistical Center, and efforts to organize freelancers through Nasr for international-internet access.
- Payamema / CITNA, 2026-04-25 (`raw/crawl21-payamema-sanandaj-internet-businesses.txt`): reports a Sanandaj Chamber knowledge-based-businesses commission meeting where local economic actors said some internet-dependent firms had neared bankruptcy and some specialist workers had become jobless; the meeting also focused on alleged operator overbilling during the international-internet cutoff.

v43 answers part of the v42 denominator problem. It still does not produce an audited national unemployment count or unemployment-insurance figure, but it does obtain source-near hiring-market records from IranTalent and JobVision. Those records show a severe demand-side freeze: opportunities and ads collapsed much more than job-seeker activity, and internet-dependent roles were the hardest hit. That pattern matters because it distinguishes "people cannot submit resumes during outage" from "employers have withdrawn jobs because the business environment is broken." The support-loan and freelancer-whitelisting sources show that the state recognizes employment damage, but its remedy is credit and selective access rather than broad restoration. Treat layoff-risk estimates and local jobless reports as corroborating impact, not a precise count.
